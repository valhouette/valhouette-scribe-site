Your brain is not malfunctioning.

It is doing exactly what it was designed to do. The problem is that it was designed for a different environment than the one it is now running inside for most of its waking hours.

This sounds like the beginning of a complaint about modern technology. It is not. It is a more precise observation: that a system built to process weighted, meaningful signals at a sustainable pace, and a system built to deliver the maximum volume of engagement-optimized content at the highest possible velocity, are not well-matched. And you are the thing that sits at their intersection, every day, trying to be a person.

---

Consider what the system was actually built for.

Not simplicity. The world that shaped your nervous system was not simple. It contained genuine danger, genuine loss, genuine social complexity. The negotiations of a small community. The weight of decisions made with people who mattered and could not be escaped. The texture of a physical world that required full-body attention to navigate.

What it did not contain was volume at the scale and velocity that now constitutes a normal morning. It was not built for thousands of curated inputs before breakfast. It was built for signal. Specific, weighted, meaningful signal that arrived at a pace the architecture could absorb without losing itself in the process.

The modern environment is not optimized for your nervous system. It is optimized for something else entirely.

---

When you plug a system built for signal into an environment optimized for volume, the system adapts.

It cannot afford to process every incoming stimulus at full emotional intensity. If it did, the result would not be insight. It would be collapse. So it drops a barrier. It lowers the sensitivity across the board. It protects you from being overwhelmed by making everything slightly less penetrating, including the things you actually wanted to be penetrated by.

This is not weakness. It is the system working correctly given the inputs it has been given.

The consequence is that you carry the adaptation out of the network and into your actual life. The barrier does not discriminate. It goes up against a high-resolution video stream and it goes up against the person sitting at the dinner table with you, and it cannot tell the difference.

---

The mismatch is not your fault and it is not the technology's fault, in the simple causal sense. It is the result of an environment evolving faster than the hardware running inside it can follow.

What matters is not assigning blame. What matters is recognizing the mismatch clearly enough to do something about it.

Because the mismatch is not fixed. The nervous system is plastic. It adapted in one direction, under one set of conditions, and it can adapt back under different ones. It does not require years. It requires sustained change in the inputs.

The hardware is not broken. It is miscalibrated. And a calibration problem, unlike a structural one, has a practical response.

The response is to change what you are running the hardware on. Not permanently. Not dramatically. Long enough for the system to register that the conditions have genuinely changed.

It is waiting to find out what you are going to give it.
