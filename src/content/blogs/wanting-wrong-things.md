At some point, you started wanting things that were never yours to want.

This is not a comfortable observation. The wanting feels genuine. It arrives with conviction. There is nothing in the experience of wanting something that announces its origin. You do not feel the mechanism. You feel the desire. And desire, by its nature, presents itself as self-evident.

But sit with this question long enough and something loosens: how much of what you are reaching toward was assembled for you rather than by you?

---

The feed is an extraordinarily efficient desire-generation engine.

Not through manipulation in the crude sense. Through exposure. Through the sustained, sophisticated presentation of lives, objects, relationships, and outcomes at a volume and velocity that makes them feel normative. Like the baseline against which your own life is being compared. Like the standard from which you are, perpetually, slightly short.

The specific desires vary by person. The architecture is constant. The system shows you what others like you appear to want, rewards you for engaging with it, and makes the alternative, the life you were living before you looked, feel slightly insufficient by comparison.

Over time, you stop noticing the comparison is happening. You simply notice the wanting.

---

There is a difference between a desire that grew inside you and a desire that was installed.

The grown desire has a root system. It connects backward to something real in your history. A value. An experience. A moment where something mattered enough to leave a trace. When you trace it backward, it leads somewhere authentic. It holds under examination.

The installed desire does not hold under examination. When you trace it backward, it leads to content. To an image that performed well. To a lifestyle that appeared in your feed during a week when you were feeling slightly restless and slightly receptive. It does not connect to anything real in you. It connects to an algorithm's model of what people like you tend to click.

This distinction matters because you will organize real time, real energy, and real choices around whatever you believe you want. If what you want was assembled for you, you will spend your life pursuing it and arriving somewhere that does not feel like yours.

---

The uncomfortable version of this question is not whether some of your desires were influenced by the feed. They were, because everyone's were, and influence is not the same as installation.

The uncomfortable version is: which ones are directing your life right now?

The apartment you are saving toward. The body you are working toward. The career shape you are trying to achieve. The relationship you are measuring yours against. Where did those originate?

This is not an invitation to abandon your ambitions. It is an invitation to examine them. Genuinely. Not in the way that produces a satisfying conclusion quickly and lets you move on, but in the slow, uncomfortable way that might reveal something you would rather not see.

Because a desire that does not belong to you can still consume you entirely. The loop does not care whether you wanted it before it started running.

You are the only one who can locate the difference.
