Your hand moves toward the phone before the rest of you has really arrived in the day. Not because you decided to. It just happens, the way a habit runs ahead of a decision.

That automatic quality is exactly why it's worth noticing. A reflex moves before the part of you that could ask whether this is actually what you want has had a chance to weigh in.

---

Before your eyes have adjusted, before your body has settled into the temperature and quiet of the room, the day is often already being filled by someone else's stream. Not your own morning thoughts. A feed. And later, it's easy to wonder why the whole day feels a little borrowed.

There was a simpler way to wake up once. Not a heroic ritual, just a few minutes where your own mind got to surface first. A window for noticing the residue of sleep, for gathering yesterday into some kind of shape before anything else asked for your attention.

---

That window still exists. It's just gotten rare enough that a lot of people don't remember what it feels like.

What seems to happen over time is a kind of training: the body starts to expect stimulation as the very first thing consciousness receives. Quiet starts to feel less like rest and more like something to get through as quickly as possible. A silent room stops feeling neutral and starts feeling like it's missing something.

Nothing is actually missing. The quiet was never empty. It was just unfamiliar.

---

Tonight, try leaving the phone across the room instead of next to the bed. Not as a permanent rule. Just for one night.

See what your mind reaches for instead, once reaching for the phone isn't the easiest option in the room.
