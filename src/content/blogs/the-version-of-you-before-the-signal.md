title: "The Version of You Before the Signal"
description: "Your life looks fine from the outside. The job continues, the messages get answered. So why does it feel thin underneath?"
publishDate: 2024-06-24

---

There is a version of you that existed before your phone became the first thing you touched every morning. It did not check anything. It did not measure a day by how much it produced or how many people saw it. That version is not gone. It is just buried under a lot of load.

You probably didn't come looking for this because your life fell apart. From the outside, it still works. Bills get paid. People still answer when you text them. The routine holds.

And yet something underneath it has gone quiet in a way that's hard to name.

---

It isn't collapse. It isn't a crisis you could point to and explain to a friend. It's more like thinness. A sense of being present in your own life without being fully inside it, the way you can be in a room and still feel like you're watching it from a few feet away.

That feeling resists easy naming because nothing dramatic caused it. No single day. No single decision. Just years of small redirections, most of them so ordinary you never noticed them happening.

---

Here is one way to think about it. Attention is not infinite, but it behaves as if it should be spent constantly. Every open moment gets filled almost automatically, a queue, a scroll, a notification, before you've had a chance to ask what you actually wanted to do with that moment.

Over time, the muscle that notices your own state before reacting to it gets less exercise. Not destroyed. Just quieter, and a little slower to respond.

---

None of this is a verdict on you. It's closer to a description of what tends to happen to attention that spends most of its hours inside systems built to hold it rather than serve it. That's a difference worth sitting with: held versus served.

The version of you that existed before the signal isn't a fantasy from childhood or some idealized past. It's closer than that. It shows up in the ten seconds after you put the phone down and just breathe in a room, before your mind starts looking for the next thing to check.

That's not nothing. That's a beginning.
