Notice where you go first when something big happens. A loss, a decision, a moment that needs more than a quick reaction. For a lot of people, the first move isn't stillness anymore. It's a screen.

That's not really a story about losing faith. It looks more like a redirection. The part of you that used to sit with a hard question until something formed out of the quiet now opens an app and waits for the answer to arrive from somewhere else.

---

There's an old relationship humans had with not-knowing. You held a question. You didn't resolve it immediately. You let it sit until something in you had matured enough to meet it.

Most digital spaces are not built for that kind of patience. An open question is a person who might click away, so the loop gets closed fast, whether or not it's actually finished. A reflection becomes a caption. A moment of doubt becomes a definitive answer served in under a second.

The instinct to search for meaning hasn't disappeared. It's just landing in places built to resolve things quickly rather than deeply.

---

This can leave you in a strange spot: fed and hollow in the same hour. You consumed something that felt spiritual, a quote, a reel, a thread that seemed to understand you. And still, an hour later, nothing in you has actually shifted.

Recognition isn't the same as transformation. You can feel briefly moved and remain exactly the person you were before you opened the app.

---

None of this is an argument to throw your phone in a drawer forever. It's simpler than that. Meaning tends to need conditions that are easy to skip past: some silence, some duration, a mind that isn't constantly interrupted before it can finish a thought.

The next time you feel that pull to search for an answer the second a hard feeling shows up, try waiting. Not forever. Just long enough to see what your own mind does with the space, before you hand the question to someone else.
