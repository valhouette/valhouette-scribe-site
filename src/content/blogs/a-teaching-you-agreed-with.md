title: "A Teaching You Agreed With"

---

You are making tea when the teacher begins to speak about surrender.

The voice is unhurried. The words are good. You find yourself nodding in a kitchen no one else is in, and for a few minutes something loosens. You save the clip. You may even send it to someone.

Then it ends, and the kitchen is exactly as it was.

---

Agreement has a particular warmth. It feels like movement, like a small step toward the person you intend to be. Because the feeling is real, it is easy not to ask what, if anything, has been done with it.

A teaching can be heard, admired and filed without ever touching a decision. The conversation you have been avoiding is still waiting. The habit you meant to examine is still in place. You have encountered an account of surrender. Whether you have surrendered anything is a separate question, and it rarely gets asked while the recording is still pleasant.

---

None of this is an argument against listening. Most practices begin with someone else's voice, and a great deal is learned that way. The difficulty is only that listening is comfortable, and comfort has a way of presenting itself as arrival.

A spectator can admire without being implicated. The teacher takes the risk of saying the words. The listener takes the pleasure of hearing them.

---

Try asking a smaller question than whether it was true.

What did I do differently on the day after I heard it? Not in public. In one ordinary decision that nobody saw.

If the honest answer is nothing yet, that is not a failure. It is the exact place where an inquiry can begin.

A person can know a great deal about surrender and remain as defended as before. Noticing that is already a small act of taking part.
