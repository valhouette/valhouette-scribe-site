title: "The Habit of Checking Before You Trust Yourself"

publishDate: 2024-08-23

---

There's a specific kind of discomfort that feels distinct from ordinary uncertainty: not quite trusting your own read of things until it's been confirmed somewhere else first.

You have a sense of what you think. A position, even a clear one. But before you act on it or say it out loud, you check. You search for something that either confirms it or replaces it. Only after that ratification does the position feel solid enough to stand on.

The position was already yours. Somewhere along the way, without anyone teaching it directly, checking first became the default.

---

Think of it as an adaptation rather than a flaw. When an outside tool consistently handles something well, the internal version of that skill tends to get used less, and skills that get used less tend to get a little rustier. Navigation is an easy example: fewer people hold routes in their heads now that a device will always do it for them.

Something similar can happen with judgment. Not the absence of judgment, more a loss of confidence in it before it's been externally approved.

---

Part of what makes this subtle is that the tools involved often sound finished already. A search result gives you raw material you still have to weigh. A more polished response can feel like the weighing has already happened for you. That shifts the psychology a little. Usefulness starts to feel like authority, and authority, once absorbed, can start to feel like permission to stop thinking first.

---

There's a simple, if not always easy, way to work against this drift: let your own read arrive first, even if it's incomplete or a little unsure. Then use the outside tool to test it, sharpen it, or challenge it, rather than to generate it for you in the first place.

That order matters more than it seems. It's the difference between using a tool and quietly becoming dependent on one.
